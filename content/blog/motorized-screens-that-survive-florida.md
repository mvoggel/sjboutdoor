A motorized screen is one of the best upgrades you can make to a Florida lanai —
shade on demand, bugs out, breeze in, and a clean look when it's rolled away. But
the gap between a screen that shrugs off a summer of afternoon storms and one
that's jammed by year three comes down to a handful of choices most homeowners
never hear about. Here's what we look at on every job.

## 1. Wind rating is the whole ballgame

In Southwest Florida, a screen that isn't rated for real wind is a screen that
will eventually fail at the worst possible moment. Ask for the **tested wind
speed** and how it was measured. We spec systems with reinforced side tracks and
a positive-retention mesh — the fabric stays locked in the track instead of
blowing out — because that's the part that gives way first when a gust hits.

> If a quote doesn't mention a wind rating at all, that's your answer about how
> the screen will hold up.

## 2. The motor is the part you'll never think about — until you do

Spend the money on a reputable tubular motor (Somfy and the like) with a real
warranty. The cheap ones save you a little up front and cost you a service call
the first humid summer. A good motor also gives you quiet operation and the
option to tie into a sun or wind sensor that retracts the screen automatically
when a storm rolls in.

## 3. Mesh: pick for the job, not the brochure photo

There's no single "best" mesh — there's the right one for what you want:

- **Insect mesh** — maximum airflow and view, keeps the no-see-ums out.
- **Solar mesh (90–95% block)** — knocks down heat and glare on a west-facing
  lanai while keeping the view.
- **Privacy / blackout** — turns a porch into a room.

We'll often put solar mesh on the sun side and insect mesh where the view
matters. You don't have to choose one for the whole house.

## 4. Side channels and the "light gap"

Look at how the screen meets the track on the sides. A quality system uses a
zipper-edge or retention spline so there's no gap for bugs, wind, or light to
sneak through. Cheaper screens leave a visible gap on each side that widens as
the fabric relaxes over time.

## 5. Who installs it matters as much as what they install

A screen is only as square as the opening it's hung in, and Florida lanais are
rarely perfectly square. The measure-and-install is where a job is won or lost.
We measure every opening ourselves, build to that opening, and stand behind the
work after the crew leaves.
